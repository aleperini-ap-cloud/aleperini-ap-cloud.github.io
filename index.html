Alessandro Perini

PhD student in Industrial Engineering, University of Rome Tor Vergata

Alessandro Perini received a BSc in Mechanical Engineering (2022) at Roma Tre University, and an MSc in Mechatronics Engineering (2024, cum laude) at the University of Rome Tor Vergata. During his MSc, he worked on the design of tendon-driven hand exoskeletons for stroke rehabilitation.

His PhD research focuses on wearable robotics with a textile substrate, integrating active elements such as tendons or smart materials as well as biomechanical sensors, developed in collaboration with Sensor Medica. In 2026, he was a visiting student at the ARIES laboratory at MIRMI, Technical University of Munich, developing a motion assistance device for the lower limbs.

Research

The work centers on wearable robots with a textile substrate: soft exoskeletons that combine active elements, such as tendons or smart materials, with biomechanical sensors. Applications range from hand rehabilitation after stroke to motion assistance for the lower limbs.

Textile hand exoskeleton

TEXO

A lightweight, accessible glove exoskeleton, fully made of sustainable textiles. It is tendon-driven, and its parametric patterns allow each glove to be tailored to the user.

  • Textiles
  • Tendon-driven
  • Parametric design

Related: IEEE T-RO paper (under review) · Italian patent application

Tendon-driven actuation

Underactuated hand exoskeletons

Adaptive tendon-driven hand exoskeletons for stroke rehabilitation, from the MSc thesis to the study of underactuation, including the design of a single-input, five-output differential actuation unit for underactuated hands.

  • Stroke rehabilitation
  • Underactuation
  • Differential actuation

Related: RoboSoft 2025 · Springer chapter 2026 · ICRA 2026

Benchmarking

Open benchmark for soft hand exoskeletons

Reproducible metrics and an open benchmark for the grasping characterization of soft hand exoskeletons.

  • Grasp characterization
  • Reproducibility
  • Metrics

Related: Advanced Intelligent Systems (under review)

Sensing and assistance

Lower-limb assistance and human sensing

A 2026 visiting period at the ARIES lab (MIRMI, Technical University of Munich) focused on a motion assistance device for the lower limbs. Related work covers barometric fusion for continuous variable assistance in a soft knee exoskeleton, and muscle activation detection from surface electromyography.

  • Soft knee exoskeleton
  • Barometric sensing
  • EMG

Related: IEEE RA-L (in preparation) · IJARS 2026
